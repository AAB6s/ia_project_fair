# Tunisia Reference: Organic Law No. 58 of 2017

Jurisdiction: Tunisia. Verify current official Arabic or French text, amendments, implementing regulations, court practice, and procedure before legal action.

Use rule:

- Use this document for Tunisia-focused violence-against-women triage.
- Do not invent article numbers. If an article number is not listed here or in another retrieved source, say the article number is not available in the retrieved sources.
- Do not cite the Tunisian Civil Code unless a retrieved source specifically discusses it.
- Cite the Tunisian Penal Code only where the retrieved source discusses Penal Code amendments or replaced Penal Code provisions.

Core law and scope:

- Organic Law No. 58 of 2017 concerns elimination of violence against women.
- Article 1 states the law uses a comprehensive approach against violence against women, including prevention, prosecution, punishment, protection, and victim support.
- Article 2 states the law concerns forms of discrimination and violence suffered by women based on sex discrimination, regardless of author or field/domain.
- Article 3 defines key terms. It treats a woman as any female person of any age. It defines violence against women broadly to include physical, moral or psychological, sexual, economic, and threat/coercion or deprivation-of-rights harms in public or private life.

Forms of violence under Article 3:

- Physical violence: harmful acts or abuse affecting physical integrity, physical safety, or life.
- Moral or psychological violence: verbal or behavioral aggression, intimidation, humiliation, threats, deprivation of rights and freedoms, or conduct aimed at domination.
- Sexual violence: acts or words aimed at subjecting a woman to sexual desires by coercion, deception, pressure, or similar means, regardless of the relationship with the author.
- Economic violence: acts or omissions that exploit women or deprive them of economic resources, including control of income, deprivation of salary or income, preventing work, or forcing work.
- Political violence: sex-discrimination-based acts or practices that prevent political, partisan, associative, or fundamental rights activity.

Victim protection and support:

- Article 13 lists rights for a woman victim of violence and children residing with her. These include appropriate legal protection, information, legal advice, judicial aid, fair reparation where execution against the offender is impossible, health and psychological follow-up, social accompaniment, and immediate accommodation within available means.
- Article 14 requires alerts to competent authorities when a person knows, observes, or notices effects of violence covered by the law. Good-faith alerts are protected from prosecution, and the identity of the alerting person must not be disclosed except with consent or where legal procedures require it.

Protection request:

- Article 30 states that the family judge may examine a protection request following a written petition from the victim or representative, the public prosecutor with the victim's consent, or the child-protection delegate where the victim is a child or a child exists. The family judge may also act on his or her own initiative. A protection request does not prevent the victim from bringing civil or criminal proceedings before competent courts.
- Article 31 states that a protection request should include the grounds, requested measures, their duration, and, where relevant, maintenance and housing allowance requests.

Services and institutions:

- Article 39 states that persons responsible for protection, including judicial police, child-protection delegates, health staff, women's/family/social/education services and others, must respond without delay to assistance and protection requests. It also prioritizes alerts involving violence threatening the physical, sexual, or psychological safety of the woman and children residing with her.

Penal-law link:

- Article 15 replaces or amends listed Penal Code provisions, including provisions related to assaults and sexual offenses. Use exact Penal Code consequences only when the retrieved source contains the specific article and facts match the issue.
- Article 42 abrogates certain listed Penal Code provisions.
