# International Framework: Violence Against Women

Jurisdiction: international human rights and public health reference. This is not a substitute for local law.

UN Declaration on the Elimination of Violence against Women:

- Violence against women is understood as gender-based violence that results in, or is likely to result in, physical, sexual, or psychological harm or suffering.
- It includes threats, coercion, and arbitrary deprivation of liberty in public or private life.
- It includes violence in the family, violence in the community, and violence perpetrated or condoned by the State.

CEDAW General Recommendation No. 35:

- Gender-based violence against women is treated as a form of discrimination against women.
- It can occur in public or private spaces, including family, community, workplace, education, health services, politics, public spaces, and online or technology-mediated environments.
- State responses should address prevention, protection, prosecution, punishment, reparations, and survivor-centered services.

WHO public health context:

- Violence against women, especially intimate partner violence and sexual violence, is a major public health problem and a human rights violation.
- Intimate partner violence can include physical aggression, sexual coercion, psychological abuse, and controlling behavior.
- Sexual violence can include coerced sexual acts, attempted sexual acts, unwanted sexual touching, and other conduct directed against a person's sexuality using coercion.
- Health consequences can include injury, pregnancy-related harm, sexually transmitted infections, depression, post-traumatic stress, anxiety, sleep problems, and elevated suicide risk.

CDC intimate partner violence context:

- Intimate partner violence is abuse or aggression in a romantic relationship and includes current or former spouses and dating partners.
- It can include physical violence, sexual violence, stalking, and psychological aggression.
- Risk factors may exist at individual, relationship, community, and societal levels. A risk factor is not proof of causation or guilt.

RAG use:

- Use this document for definitions, risk context, survivor-centered framing, and safety issues.
- Do not use this document alone to conclude what legal claim exists in a specific jurisdiction.
- Always retrieve local law, procedure, and evidence requirements when making legal recommendations.
