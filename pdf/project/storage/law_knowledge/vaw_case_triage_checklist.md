# Violence Against Women Case Triage Checklist

Purpose: retrieval checklist for the legal feedback engine. It helps identify facts, evidence gaps, and safe next steps. It is not legal advice.

Immediate safety:

- Is anyone in immediate danger?
- Are there threats of homicide, suicide, kidnapping, weapons, strangulation, confinement, stalking escalation, or child danger?
- Is there an existing protection order or no-contact order?
- Has the order been violated?
- Does the survivor need emergency medical care, shelter, transport, or safe communication?

Jurisdiction:

- Where did each incident occur?
- Where do the parties live?
- Are there federal, state, tribal, territorial, local, or Tunisia-specific issues?
- Are children, immigration status, housing, employment, school, or military status involved?

Relationship:

- Current or former spouse.
- Current or former intimate partner.
- Dating partner.
- Cohabitant or former cohabitant.
- Shared child.
- Family or household member.
- Non-partner sexual violence or stalking.

Conduct:

- Physical injury or attempted injury.
- Sexual violence or coercion.
- Threats, intimidation, coercive control, isolation, humiliation.
- Economic abuse or exploitation.
- Technological abuse, monitoring, tracking, impersonation, extortion, image-based abuse.
- Stalking, repeated unwanted contact, following, surveillance.
- Damage to property, pets, documents, phone, accounts, or residence.

Evidence:

- Medical records, photographs, injury timeline, hospital or clinic notes.
- Police reports, incident numbers, court documents, protection orders.
- Text messages, emails, call logs, voicemails, social media, screenshots, exports with metadata.
- Witness names and statements.
- Housing notices, lease documents, school or workplace records.
- Financial records, bank statements, wage records, debt, benefit control.
- Device/account logs, location data, spyware indicators, login alerts.

Model-derived document review:

- If a document is classified as injury_photo, scene_damage, medical_record, signature_stamp, correspondence, or typed_document, check whether it supports core facts.
- If quality is degraded, ask for better scans or originals.
- If tamper risk is high, ask for original files, source metadata, independent verification, or official records.
- If evidence weight is secondary, do not rely on it alone for a legal conclusion.

Recommended output behavior:

- Separate facts from legal issues.
- State missing facts clearly.
- Cite retrieved chunks.
- Recommend verification with local counsel.
- Avoid giving definitive legal claims when jurisdiction, dates, or statutory elements are missing.

Source basis:

- DOJ OVW VAWA and focus areas.
- 34 U.S.C. 12291 definitions.
- VAWA housing and confidentiality provisions.
- UN DEVAW and CEDAW General Recommendation No. 35.
- WHO and CDC intimate partner violence references.
