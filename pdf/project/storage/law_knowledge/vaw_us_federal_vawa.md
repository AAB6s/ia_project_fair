# United States Federal Reference: VAWA and OVW

Jurisdiction: United States federal. State, tribal, territorial, local, family, criminal, housing, immigration, and evidence rules may add or change procedures.

Primary source: DOJ Office on Violence Against Women and 34 U.S.C. 12291.

Core VAWA context:

- The Violence Against Women Act was first passed in 1994 and later reauthorized, including in 2000, 2005, 2013, and 2022.
- DOJ OVW administers VAWA-authorized programs and focuses on domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, and stalking.
- OVW does not provide direct services to the public; it points people to local resources, emergency services, and national hotlines.

Domestic violence:

- Domestic violence includes covered crimes by a current or former spouse or intimate partner and, for victim services, also includes physical abuse, sexual abuse, and patterns of coercive behavior used to gain or maintain power and control.
- Coercive behavior can include verbal, psychological, economic, or technological abuse, even when some behavior may not itself be criminal.
- Relevant relationships include current or former spouse/intimate partner, cohabitant or former cohabitant, shared child, or similarly situated person under the applicable jurisdiction.

Dating violence:

- Dating violence involves violence by a person who is or was in a romantic or intimate social relationship with the victim.
- The relationship is evaluated by length, type, and frequency of interaction.

Stalking:

- Stalking is a course of conduct directed at a specific person that would cause a reasonable person to fear for safety or suffer substantial emotional distress.
- Stalking may overlap with technology-facilitated abuse, monitoring, repeated contact, threats, impersonation, location tracking, or harassment.

Sexual assault and sexual violence:

- For triage, sexual assault evidence can involve medical records, forensic exam records, police reports, victim statements, digital communications, witness statements, and injury or scene documentation.
- Consent, force, coercion, incapacity, age, relationship, and jurisdiction-specific definitions must be verified.

Technology-facilitated abuse:

- 34 U.S.C. 12291 recognizes technological abuse as conduct within domestic violence, sexual assault, dating violence, or stalking intended to harm, threaten, intimidate, control, stalk, harass, impersonate, exploit, extort, or monitor another person through technology.
- Evidence may include screenshots, device logs, location tracking, message metadata, social media posts, account access history, spyware indicators, and camera or app records.

Practical retrieval cues:

- Ask whether there is a current danger, protection order, police report, medical record, child safety issue, immigration issue, housing issue, workplace/school issue, or digital surveillance.
- Do not assume criminal liability from one document. Identify facts, dates, injuries, threats, relationship, location, jurisdiction, and available evidence.
- Preserve originals, metadata, screenshots with timestamps, medical records, reports, and communications.
